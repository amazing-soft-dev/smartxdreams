Scam messages look more real every year. They can use a real company's name and logo. They can know your name. But most of them still follow the same few patterns.

Here are five signs to look for. One sign alone does not prove a scam. Two or more are a strong reason to stop and check.

## 1. It says you must act right now

Scammers want you to act before you think. Watch for words like "today", "final notice", "your account will be closed" or "you will be fined". A real company will give you time to check.

## 2. It asks for private details

Be careful when a message asks for your card number, your bank details, a password or a PIN. Be very careful when it asks for a code that was just sent to your phone. Your bank will not ask you for these by text, email or phone call.

## 3. It asks you to pay in an unusual way

Gift cards, money transfer apps and cryptocurrency are hard to get back. Real companies and government offices do not ask you to pay with gift cards.

## 4. You were not expecting it

A prize you did not enter for. A parcel you did not order. A refund you did not ask for. A call from someone who says they are from your bank's "security team". Unexpected messages deserve extra care.

## 5. It asks you to keep it secret

Some scammers tell you not to talk to your family or your bank. They may say it is "for your safety". A real company will never ask you to keep a problem from your family.

## What to do if something feels wrong

- **Stop.** You do not have to answer, click or pay right now.
- **Do not use the contact details in the message.** Find the company's number yourself: on your card, on a bill or on its official website.
- **Call that number and ask.** If the message was real, they can help you.
- **Talk to someone you trust.** A second opinion helps.

If you think you have already paid or shared details, call your bank straight away on the number on your card.
