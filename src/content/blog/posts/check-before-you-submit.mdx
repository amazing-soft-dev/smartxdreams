Most application forms ask for the same things: your contact details, your work history, your education, and a few questions about the role. Typing them in again for every application is slow, so autofill is tempting.

Autofill is good at copying. It is not good at judgement. Whatever tool you use, including your browser's own autofill, the application goes out under your name. These are the things worth checking every time.

## Start from one accurate profile

Autofill can only be as good as what it copies from. Keep one up-to-date profile with your history, dates, job titles and contact details exactly as they appear on your resume. If the profile has an old phone number or a job title you have since changed, every form inherits the mistake.

## Check the fields that change from form to form

Some fields look the same but mean different things on different forms:

- **Dates:** one form wants month and year, another wants a full date, another wants years of experience.
- **Job titles:** a form may offer a drop-down list that does not match your exact title. Choose the closest honest option.
- **Salary:** some forms ask for current pay, others for expected pay, and the currency or period (yearly, hourly) varies.
- **Location and right to work:** answer for this role and this country, not from memory of the last form.

## Read every screening question yourself

Screening questions often decide whether a person ever reads your application. Questions like "Do you have experience with…" or "Are you willing to…" deserve a deliberate answer. Never let a tool claim experience you do not have. It is easy to spot in an interview, and it is not fair to you or the employer.

## Tailor the parts that should be tailored

A short summary or cover note that names the company and the role reads better than a generic one. Keep a few versions for the kinds of roles you apply to, and choose or adjust the one that fits.

## Respect each site's rules

Job sites set their own terms for how their forms are used. Read them, and prefer tools that fill in a form for you to review rather than ones that submit applications automatically.
