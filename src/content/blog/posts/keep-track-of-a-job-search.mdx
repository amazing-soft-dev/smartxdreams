A job search rarely fails for lack of openings. It gets messy because the openings live in too many places. The same role shows up on several boards and a company careers page, some of those listings closed weeks ago, and by the end of an evening it is hard to say which ones you have already seen.

A little structure fixes most of that. Here is a simple system you can run with a spreadsheet, a notes app or a tool built for it.

## Decide what a good match looks like before you search

Write down three to five things a role must have: the kind of work, the level, where it is based or whether it is remote, and anything you will not compromise on. Then write a short list of things that would be nice but are not essential.

With that list in front of you, it takes seconds to skip a listing that does not fit. Without it, every listing gets a full read.

## Keep one list, not one per site

Pick one place where every role you are interested in goes, whatever site you found it on. For each role, note the company, the title, where you found it, the link to the original posting and the date you saved it.

When the same job turns up on a second site, you will see it is already on your list. Keep the link to the employer's own posting where you can: it is usually the most up to date.

## Check that a listing is still open

Before you spend time on an application, open the original posting. If it has closed, or the company's careers page no longer lists it, move on. Listings on aggregator sites can outlive the real opening.

## Give every application a next step

For each role you apply to, write down the date you applied and the next step: a date to follow up, an interview to prepare for, or a decision to wait for. A list with next steps tells you what to do today. A list without them only tells you what you did last week.

## Keep your answers in one place too

Applications ask the same questions in different forms: your work history, your notice period, why you want the role. Keep your best answers in a single document so you can reuse and adapt them instead of starting from a blank box each time.
