A dental treatment quote and a dental insurance plan are written in two different languages. The quote lists treatments, codes and fees. The plan lists coverage percentages, limits and exclusions. The number you actually want, what you will pay, appears in neither.

This guide explains how the two fit together. It is general information, not advice about your treatment or your plan. Your dentist decides what treatment you need, and your insurer decides what it pays.

## Start with the quote

Ask for a written quote that lists each treatment separately, with its fee. Many quotes also show a procedure code for each item. Keep the codes: plan documents and insurers often use them to decide how an item is covered.

## Find how each item is covered

Plans often group treatments into types, for example routine care, basic treatment such as fillings, and major treatment such as crowns, and cover each type at a different percentage. Find the section of your plan that explains these groups and match each item on the quote to one of them.

## Check the limits that can change the answer

Several common limits can change what you pay, even when an item is covered:

- **Deductible:** an amount you may pay yourself before the plan starts paying.
- **Yearly maximum:** many plans pay only up to a set amount in a plan year. Work you have already had this year counts towards it.
- **Waiting periods:** some plans do not cover certain treatments until you have been on the plan for a set time.
- **Frequency limits:** some treatments are covered only once in a set period.

Your plan document, or your insurer's member service, can tell you which of these apply and how much of this year's maximum you have used.

## Ask for an estimate from your insurer

Many insurers can give an estimate before treatment starts, often called a pre-treatment estimate or predetermination. Your dentist's office can usually send the request. It is the closest you will get to a firm number before the work is done.

## Talk to your dentist about timing

When a plan has a yearly maximum, the timing of treatment can affect your cost. Whether any part of the work can safely wait is a clinical question, so ask your dentist rather than deciding on cost alone.
