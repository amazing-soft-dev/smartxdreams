Sooner or later, a customer sends a small software company a security questionnaire. It might be a spreadsheet, a portal or a long document, and a deal often waits until it is done.

The pressure is to answer quickly and answer "yes". But each answer is a statement about how your company works, and customers may hold you to it. The goal is not the most impressive answers. It is answers that are true and that you can show evidence for.

## Gather your sources first

Before you answer anything, collect the documents that describe how you actually work: your security policy, access control and incident response procedures, backup and recovery plans, and any audit reports or certifications you hold. Note each document's version and date.

If a document is out of date, fix that first. A good answer built on an old policy is still a wrong answer.

## Answer from the documents, and cite them

For each question, find the passage that supports your answer and note where it is: the document, the section and the version. If a reviewer asks "where does it say that?", you can point to it.

Keep answers specific. "Production databases are encrypted at rest; see Security Policy §4.2" is more useful to a reviewer than "Yes, we take security seriously".

## Flag what you cannot support

Some questions will ask about controls you do not have, or have only partly. Do not round them up to "yes". Answer honestly: "No", "Partially", or "Not currently, planned for…" only if a plan really exists. Mark these for a person to decide how to answer and whether to add context.

An honest "no" with an explanation usually goes down better than a "yes" that falls apart in a follow-up call.

## Have a person approve every answer

Questionnaires mix technical and legal statements. Someone who knows how the company really works should read and approve each answer before it is sent, and anything involving contracts or certifications should go to whoever owns those.

## Keep what you send

Save the final answers with the date, the customer and the document versions they relied on. The next questionnaire will ask many of the same questions, and you will want to answer consistently.
